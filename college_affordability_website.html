01 · Understand the issue

Why does college cost so much?

College can create opportunities, but tuition and other education expenses can place a major burden on students and families. Research points to several factors—not one explanation that fits every institution.

01

Government funding

Changes in state support can affect public colleges’ financial resources and decisions about tuition and enrollment.

(Berger & Kostal, 2002; Combs, 2026)

02

College spending

Institutions face operating expenses and make choices about how to spend available resources. Those choices may differ across colleges.

(Combs, 2026; Scogin, 2025)

03

Changing prices over time

Historical tuition data helps show how college prices have changed and why affordability is an important public issue.

(Hanson, 2025; Nova, 2022)

Remember: Tuition trends vary by institution and time period. Funding and spending are important factors to investigate, but they should not be treated as the only causes of rising costs.
02 · Why it matters

The impact on students and families

The cost of college can shape decisions before a student enrolls—and throughout their education.

  • Students may choose a school based on affordability.
  • Tuition increases may affect whether students attend a two-year or four-year college.
  • Students and families may need to compare loans, grants, scholarships, and other funding options.
  • Financial pressure can make planning for education more difficult.

Research has examined the relationship between tuition and enrollment choices (Hemelt & Marcotte, 2016).

Key takeaway: College affordability is about more than the tuition price. Students should also consider fees, living costs, available aid, and whether credits transfer.
03 · Options worth exploring

What can be done?

No single solution will fit every student or college. These are options students, institutions, and policymakers can evaluate.

🎓

Strengthen access to financial aid

Help students find grants and scholarships, understand aid offers, and complete financial-aid applications.

🏛️

Examine state funding

Evaluate how public funding is distributed and how funding changes affect tuition, institutional spending, and access.

🔎

Compare affordable pathways

Compare two-year and four-year institutions, total costs, transfer agreements, and the time needed to complete a degree.

📊

Make college costs transparent

Present tuition, fees, living expenses, and likely out-of-pocket costs clearly so students can compare options.
